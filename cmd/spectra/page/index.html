Spectral Plates

Gamma-ray bursts carried into visible light by libtheme's radiation profiles. A profile maps its band onto the observer's visible range, 360 to 830 nm, evenly in the logarithm of wavelength, so every factor of ten in energy gets an equal slice of the rainbow. Higher energy lands bluer.

Through the whole Fermi profile, 10 keV to 300 GeV, the eye's most sensitive stretch, about 450 to 650 nm, stands for roughly 1.5 MeV to 3 GeV. A burst there is only its high-energy tail, and bursts share the slope of that tail, so a long burst and a short one come out the same orange and a burst's softening does not show. Through Fermi's burst monitor alone, 8 keV to 40 MeV, the peak lands inside the visible: a long burst comes out tan, a short one sea green, and a short burst softens from green to tan.

How these were made

Each burst is the Band function, with round values typical of the BATSE and Fermi catalogues. Long: low slope −1, high slope −2.3, peak 200 keV. Short: −0.5, −2.3, peak 600 keV. Its power in each of libtheme's bands goes in through bands.Lines and out through Profile.Light.

The top strip is the light as an eye would see it after the profile, with one exposure for the whole strip. The bars are each band's power, linear, drawn in its wavelength's own colour. Single colours and the softening ramp are held at one oklab lightness, 0.72, so only hue and chroma change, and are pulled into sRGB by chroma with ok.Fit. The softening ramp moves the peak from the burst's own value down to a tenth of it.
